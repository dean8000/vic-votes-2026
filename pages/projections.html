How the seat projection works

  1. Take the average Labor-vs-Coalition two-party-preferred (2PP) from the most recent 5 published polls.
  2. Compare that to Labor's actual 2022 statewide 2PP of 55.0%. The difference is the uniform swing.
  3. Apply that swing to every district's 2022 2PP result — e.g. if Labor swing is −9%, a district that was Labor 55% TPP in 2022 is projected at 46% TPP in 2026 (Coalition win).
  4. Classify each seat as safe hold (projected margin ≥5%), close hold (2–5%), in play (<2%), or likely flip (projected winner differs from sitting MP).

Why this is simplistic:

  • Real swings are rarely uniform. Some districts move more, some less, some against the trend.
  • High-profile independents (e.g. Teal) can completely rewrite the two-party dynamic in a seat.
  • Greens- and Independent-held seats aren't a Labor-vs-Coalition contest, so uniform swing doesn't apply cleanly. We flag these.
  • One Nation's rise in Victoria to >20% primary (per current polls) could redistribute preferences in ways the 2022 pattern doesn't capture.
  • Local candidate quality, campaign events, and the final weeks' media cycle all matter.
