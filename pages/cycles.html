Three different election cycles

In Victoria you vote in three completely separate elections, run by different bodies on different schedules. This page is the cheat-sheet.

Common confusion: wards and councillors belong to local council elections — not the state election. If you're asking "which ward am I in?" or "who's my councillor?" — that's local government, which was last elected in October 2024 and isn't on the ballot this November.

Why do the areas look different?

The three systems draw their boundaries independently. Your federal division, state district, and council ward rarely line up. For example, someone living in Albert Park might be in:

  • Federal division of Macnamara
  • State district of Albert Park & state region of Southern Metropolitan
  • Council: City of Port Phillip, Albert Park Ward

Each level has its own electoral commission activity. For this November's election, you'll only be voting on the state-level ones — district (lower house) and region (upper house).

What about by-elections?

If a sitting MP dies, resigns or is disqualified, a by-election is held in just that one seat. These happen whenever and don't disturb the overall cycle. For example, the Nepean state by-election was held in 2026 after the sitting MP resigned. By-elections can also happen in federal divisions and council wards.
