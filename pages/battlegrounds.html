Battlegrounds — the seats that will decide this election

Of 88 lower-house seats, perhaps 30 will actually be close. Here's a curated view of the ones most worth watching, grouped by the kind of contest they are.

These are projections, not predictions. The categories below come from applying statewide polling swing to each district's 2022 result. Real elections produce swings that vary by seat; local candidate effects, independents, and preference flows all matter. Treat as a map of what to watch, not a forecast.

How these categories were chosen

  • Tightest projected flips — Labor seats that the uniform-swing model projects to fall to the Coalition by the narrowest margins. Small polling error = these stay Labor.
  • True toss-ups — projected 2PP within 2% of 50-50. Either side could win on the night.
  • Non-two-party races — Greens-held seats, Independent-held seats, plus Labor seats where a Greens challenger is competitive (inner-metropolitan seats where Greens polled >15% in 2022). Uniform swing doesn't apply; these turn on local dynamics.
  • Open contests — the sitting MP is retiring, so there's no incumbency advantage. Historically these swing more than seats with the incumbent on the ballot.
