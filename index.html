Not sure what to do? If you're an enrolled voter in Victoria, voting is compulsory. On election day you'll fill in two ballot papers — a small green one for the lower house and a large white one for the upper house. The sections below explain how each works.

What is this election for?

You're electing the next Parliament of Victoria, which has two houses:

  • Legislative Assembly (lower house) — 88 single-member electorates (called districts).
  • Legislative Council (upper house) — 8 regions, each electing 5 members.

Both are elected on the same day, every 4 years. The last state election was 26 November 2022.

How do I actually vote?

Voting is compulsory for enrolled Victorians aged 18+. You can vote on election day, early in person, by post, or electronically if you qualify.

  • Lower house: number every box in order of preference.
  • Upper house: number at least 5 parties above the line, or at least 5 candidates below.

What's new this time

Victoria has abolished Group Voting Tickets for the upper house. Previously, if you voted "1" above the line, your preferences were allocated by the party according to backroom deals. Now you allocate your own preferences by numbering at least 5 boxes.

This brings Victoria into line with the federal Senate.

Easy to confuse — three different elections

Australians vote in three separate election cycles, each run by a different body on a different schedule. The 2026 Victorian state election is only one of them.

  • Federal (Australian) elections — run by the AEC. Last held 3 May 2025; next due by Sep 2028.
  • State (Victorian) elections — run by the VEC. This is the November 2026 one.
  • Local council elections — run by the VEC on behalf of councils. Last held October 2024; next October 2028. This is where you elect councillors for wards.

If you're thinking about "my councillor" or "my ward" — that's local council, which was decided two years ago and isn't on the ballot this November.
